# Education, bots, data and agents

@concept
## Education
When ChatGPT goes down, students flood OpenAI's Discord because they can't finish homework.

New York City and Los Angeles schools banned ChatGPT out of fear of cheating, then reversed the ban a few months later.
---
@concept
## Personalized learning
Instead of banning AI, schools can use it. AI can summarize textbooks and make a lesson plan for each student.

It can change the format for each person: read material aloud, use examples about animals for kids who love animals, or turn math equations into code for people who read code more easily.

Duolingo found that, of four stages in making a course, personalizing lessons gains the most from AI.
---
@concept
## AI as tutor and debate partner
AI can write quizzes, both multiple choice and open answers, and grade them. It is good at showing several sides of a topic, so it makes a good debate partner.

Khan Academy offers AI assistants to students and teachers. One teacher hands out AI-written essays and asks students to find the mistakes.
---
@concept
## Winners and losers
Some education companies lose to AI. Chegg, a homework-help company, fell from $28 a share when ChatGPT launched (November 2022) to $2 by September 2024.

The flip side: AI can tutor you in almost any skill, get you started quickly, and then you keep learning on your own.
---
@concept
## Conversational bots
Bots can find information, explain ideas, brainstorm, act as a companion, or imitate a personality. AI companions became very popular very quickly.

Researchers have even used groups of bots to simulate a small society and study how people interact.
---
@concept
## Bots at work and beyond text
For companies, the top bot is customer support: it replies faster than people and costs less. **Product copilots** guide users through hard tasks like insurance claims or taxes.

Bots don't have to be text. Voice assistants like Siri and Alexa have been around for years. In games, AI can make **NPCs** (non-player characters) much smarter than scripted ones.
---
@concept
## Information aggregation
Emails, Slack and news can be overwhelming. AI is good at collecting information and summarizing it. In a 2023 Salesforce survey, 74% of generative AI users used it to simplify complex ideas and summarize.

For consumers, apps let you chat with your own documents, like contracts or papers. This is called **talk-to-your-docs**.
---
@concept
## A prompt that turns chat into tasks
When Instacart opened an internal library of prompts, one of the most used was "Fast Breakdown".

It summarizes meeting notes, emails and Slack threads into facts, open questions and action items. The action items can go straight into a project tracker with the right owners.

Less time spent passing information around means less load on middle managers.
---
@concept
## Data organization
We keep making more data: photos, videos, logs, contracts, PDFs. Most of it is **unstructured** (no fixed format) or **semistructured**, and it has to be organized so it can be searched.

AI can describe images and videos in text, or match a text search to the right pictures, as Google Photos does. If nothing matches, Google Image Search can even generate an image.
---
@concept
## Pulling structure out of mess
Companies use AI to extract structured data from unstructured documents: details from credit cards, licenses, receipts, tickets and email signatures, or harder ones like contracts, reports and charts.

This field is called **IDP** (intelligent data processing). One estimate says it will reach $12.81 billion by 2030.

AI is also good at data analysis: writing code for charts, finding outliers, forecasting revenue.
---
@concept
## Workflow automation
In the end, AI should automate as much as possible.

For people: booking restaurants, asking for refunds, planning trips, filling forms.

For companies: managing leads, invoices, reimbursements, customer requests, data entry. AI can even create and label data that is then used to improve AI models, with humans checking the labels.
---
@concept
## Agents
Many tasks need outside tools. To book a restaurant, an app might search the web for the number, make a call, and add the booking to your calendar.

An AI that can plan and use tools is called an **agent**. Interest in agents is close to obsession, but for a reason: they could make each person far more productive. Chapter 6 covers them.
