# Three reasons AI engineering grew

@concept
## Why a new name now?
Teams have built ML applications for over ten years. That work is called **ML engineering** or **MLOps** (ML operations).

The key difference: traditional ML engineering builds models. AI engineering uses existing ones.

Strong, easy-to-reach foundation models created three conditions that made AI engineering grow fast.
---
@concept
## Factor 1: general-purpose abilities
Foundation models don't just do old tasks better. They do more tasks, including some no one thought possible.

AI can now write about as well as people, so it can help with almost anything that involves communication: emails, customer replies, explaining contracts.

Anyone can make custom images and video. AI can even create training data and write code, which helps build better models.
---
@concept
## Factor 2: more investment
ChatGPT's success brought a wave of money from investors and companies.

As AI apps got cheaper and faster to build, the return looked better. At Scribd, the estimated AI cost for their use cases fell about 100 times between April 2022 and April 2023.

Goldman Sachs estimated AI investment could reach about $100 billion in the US and $200 billion worldwide by 2025.
---
@concept
## Everyone talks about AI
In the second quarter of 2023, one in three S&P 500 companies mentioned AI in earnings calls, three times more than a year earlier.

Companies that mentioned AI saw their stock rise more: 4.6% on average, against 2.4%.

But that may be **correlation**, not **causation**. Maybe AI made them succeed, or maybe successful companies are just quick to adopt new technology.
---
@concept
## Factor 3: a low barrier to entry
With model as a service, a model sits behind an API. You send a request, you get its output. One API call gives you a powerful model, with no servers to host it.

It goes further. AI can write the code for you, and you talk to models in plain English instead of a programming language.

So almost anyone can build an AI application now.
---
@concept
## Few build, many adapt
Building foundation models takes so much that only a few can: big companies (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Baidu, Tencent), some governments (Japan, the UAE), and well-funded startups (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral).

In 2022, OpenAI's CEO Sam Altman said the biggest opportunity for most people is adapting these models to specific applications.
---
@concept
## Signs of fast growth
Within two years, four open source AI tools (AutoGPT, Stable Diffusion web UI, LangChain, Ollama) got more GitHub stars than Bitcoin. They were on track to pass React and Vue.

A LinkedIn survey from August 2023 found that people adding terms like "Generative AI" and "Prompt Engineering" to their profiles grew about 75% each month.
---
@concept
## Why "AI engineering" and not another name?
Not "ML engineering": working with foundation models differs from traditional ML in important ways.

Not a name ending in "Ops" (MLOps, LLMOps): there is operations work, but the main job is engineering models to do what you want.

And when the author asked 20 people building on foundation models what they'd call it, most chose "AI engineering".
