# Coding, images and writing

@concept
## Coding comes first
In survey after survey, coding is the most popular use of generative AI.

Two reasons: AI is good at code, and the early AI engineers were programmers who knew coding pain firsthand.
---
@concept
## Copilot and friends
GitHub Copilot was one of the first big production hits. Its yearly revenue passed $100 million only two years after launch.

AI coding startups have raised hundreds of millions of dollars. In August 2024, Magic raised $320 million and Anysphere $60 million. Open source tools like gpt-engineer and screenshot-to-code reached 50,000 GitHub stars within a year.
---
@concept
## Tools for specific coding jobs
Beyond general coding help, many tools target one job:

Pulling structured data from web pages and PDFs.

Turning English into code or SQL.

Turning a design or screenshot into a working web page.

Translating from one language or framework to another.

Writing documentation, writing tests, and writing commit messages.
---
@concept
## Will AI replace programmers?
Opinions sit at two ends.

Some leaders, like NVIDIA's CEO, say AI will replace programmers and kids shouldn't learn to code. AWS's CEO said most developers will soon stop coding, meaning the job will change, not vanish.

At the other end, many engineers say AI will never take their job. Some of that is technical, and some is that nobody likes to admit they could be replaced.
---
@concept
## Better at some tasks than others
McKinsey found AI made developers about twice as fast at documentation, and 25 to 50% faster at writing and refactoring code.

For very complex tasks, the gain was small.

Builders of AI coding tools also say AI is much better at frontend work than backend work.
---
@concept
## Fewer people, more work
Whether or not AI replaces engineers, it makes them more productive. Companies can do more with fewer engineers.

It can also shake up outsourcing, since outsourced work is often simpler work outside a company's core.
---
@concept
## Images and video
AI is probabilistic: it doesn't give the same answer every time. That suits creative work.

Some of the biggest AI startups are creative: Midjourney for images, Adobe Firefly for photo editing, Runway, Pika Labs and Sora for video. Midjourney reached $200 million in yearly revenue when it was only a year and a half old.
---
@concept
## From banned to built in
Views on AI profile photos flipped fast. Facebook banned them in 2019 over safety. By 2023, many social apps had built-in tools to create them.

For companies, ads and marketing adopted AI fast. AI can create promo images and videos, brainstorm, and draft ideas for experts to refine. You can make several ads and test which one works best. You can also make versions for each season or place, like adding snow for winter.
---
@concept
## Writing: we forgive mistakes
Phone autocorrect and autocomplete were AI writing aids long before ChatGPT.

Writing suits AI well: we write a lot, it can be tedious, and mistakes cost little. If you don't like a suggestion, you just ignore it.

And LLMs are trained to complete text, so writing is their home turf.
---
@concept
## What the MIT study found
An MIT study (2023) gave writing tasks to 453 professionals and gave ChatGPT to half of them.

With ChatGPT, tasks took 40% less time and quality rose 18%.

It helped weaker writers most, so it narrowed the gap between people.
---
@concept
## Writing for people and companies
People use AI to make an angry email polite, or to turn bullet points into paragraphs. Students write essays with it, and some startups generate whole books, even interactive ones whose plot follows the reader.

Companies use it for sales emails, ad copy, product descriptions and reports. Grammarly finetunes a model to make writing clearer.
---
@concept
## The dark side: junk content
AI writing can be abused. In 2023, Amazon was flooded with low-quality AI travel guides, with fake author bios and fake reviews.

AI is very good at **SEO** (writing to rank high in search), maybe because it learned from SEO-heavy web text. Content farms fill junk sites with AI text to attract traffic and sell ads. One such site published 1,200 articles a day.
